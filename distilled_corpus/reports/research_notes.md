# Research Notes — Multi-Teacher Distillation for a 1–2B Decoder-Only Model

All claims below are grounded in primary sources (papers, official tech reports, official
repos/model cards). Dates 2024–2026. Used to justify design decisions in this pipeline.

## 1. Token-budget / scaling laws for small models

- **Chinchilla** (Hoffmann et al., 2022, arXiv:2203.15556): compute-optimal training uses
  ~20 tokens per parameter. For a 1–2B model this is ~20–40B tokens **for compute-optimal
  pretraining from scratch on web data**. This is an *upper* guide, not a minimum.
- **Phi-1 / Phi-1.5** (Gunasekar et al., 2023, arXiv:2306.11644; Li et al., 2023,
  arXiv:2309.05463): 1.3B models trained on **~100B "textbook quality" tokens** (heavily
  synthetic, curated) reach GPT-3.5-level coding/reasoning. Key result: **data quality and
  knowledge density matter far more than raw token count** for small models. "Textbook is
  all you need."
- **Phi-2 / Phi-3** (Microsoft, 2023–2024): Phi-3-mini (3.8B) trained on 3.3T tokens of
  heavily filtered/synthetic data matches much larger models. Synthetic data is a major
  component, generated from larger teacher models with careful filtering.
- **Phi-4** (arXiv:2412.08905, Dec 2024): Explicitly uses **synthetic data from multiple
  teacher models + curricular data + midtraining on STEM/code**. Phi-4 (14B) beats
  instruction-tuned 70B-class models on STEM. Demonstrates high-quality synthetic
  orchestration (AST-based filtering, execution-based filtering for code, multi-key
  de-duplication). Directly supports this project's multi-teacher approach.
- **Practical implication**: For 1–2B, a *curated* 0.5–2B-token corpus can outperform a
  10–20B raw-web corpus. Target density over volume.

## 2. Knowledge distillation methods (LLM→LLM)

- **DistilBERT / distillation classics** (Sanh et al. 2019): logit/hidden MSE
  distillation. For pretraining-from-scratch, *logit* distillation requires teacher
  logits at every token (teacher in the loop) — expensive and teacher-locked. Not what we
  do here.
- **Sequence-level / response distillation** (Taori et al. 2023 "Stanford Alpaca";
  Chiang et al. 2023 "Vicuna"; Mukherjee et al. 2023 "Orca", arXiv:2306.02707): train
  student on **teacher-generated text**. Orca showed progressive complexity signals and
  explanation tracing from GPT-4 improve small models. This is the paradigm we use:
  **text-level distillation**, not logit distillation.
- **GKD — Generalized Knowledge Distillation** (Agarwal et al., 2023, arXiv:2306.13649):
  *on-policy* distillation where the student samples and the teacher provides the
  target distribution/logits. Strong but requires teacher logits during training.
  Relevant as a *later* stage; not for corpus generation now.
- **Distilling step-by-step** (Hsieh et al., 2023, arXiv:2305.02338): extract
  rationales from a large teacher, train a small student on both task + rationale. Supports
  including concise derivations in the corpus.
- **DeepSeek-R1 distillation** (DeepSeek, Jan 2025, arXiv:2501.12948): R1's reasoning
  distilled into small dense models (1.5B, 7B, 8B, 14B, 32B, 70B Qwen/Llama bases) by
  **fine-tuning on 800K R1-generated reasoning samples**. Small distilled models beat
  much larger non-reasoning models on math/code. Key: reasoning traces were *generated by
  the strong reasoning teacher* and filtered. Demonstrates value of reasoning-distillation
  data but also that **verbose raw CoT is not always necessary** — concise, verifiable
  derivations transfer well.

## 3. Reasoning representation in training corpus

- **Concise vs verbose CoT**: DeepSeek-R1 distillation uses full traces; Phi-4 uses
  structured/curated reasoning. Research (e.g., "Let's Verify Step by Step", Lightman
  et al. 2023, arXiv:2305.20050; "Quiet-STaR", Zelikman et al. 2024) shows **process
  rewards on verifiable steps** beat unfiltered long traces. Implication: store
  **concise, verifiable derivations** (proofs, explicit calculations, structured steps
  with intermediate conclusions) rather than rambling private monologue. Avoid making the
  entire corpus chain-of-thought.
- **Format**: store reasoning as structured natural-language documents (worked examples,
  proofs, step lists), not as `<think>...</think>` chat artifacts.

## 4. Synthetic-data failure modes

- **Model collapse** (Shumailov et al., 2023/2024, arXiv:2311.09233, Nature 2024):
  training generation-after-generation on synthetic data from models causes performance
  degradation and loss of tail distributions. **Mitigation**: keep real/source-grounded
  data in the mix; never do multi-generation synthetic loops without fresh real seeds;
  measure diversity; deduplicate aggressively.
- **Synthetic-data feedback loops / mode collapse**: repeated paraphrasing → stylistic
  homogenization. **Mitigation**: vary prompts, temperatures, teachers, document formats;
  detect near-duplicates; reject templated phrasing.
- **Hallucination & factual drift**: teachers hallucinate facts, citations, code.
  **Mitigation**: multi-teacher agreement + execution/symbolic verification + reject
  uncertain claims (this pipeline).
- **Benchmark contamination**: teachers may have memorized benchmarks; synthetic Q's can
  leak into eval. **Mitigation**: original held-out eval set, separate from training,
  never fed to teachers as training seeds.
- **Teacher-style overfitting**: student copies "As an AI..." / "According to DeepSeek..."
  phrasing. **Mitigation**: style-normalization pass that strips teacher identity/meta
  language.

## 5. Data mixture evidence

- **Phi-1.5/2/3**: ~heavily synthetic + textbooks; balanced code+text+STEM.
- **DeepSeek pretraining** (V3, arXiv:2412.19437): pretraining corpus ~14.8T tokens
  with curated mix; dedup at multiple granularities; "fill-in-the-middle" for code.
- **Qwen2.5 tech report** (arXiv:2412.15115): large-scale pretraining with
  high-quality multilingual data, strong code/math proportions, aggressive dedup and
  quality filtering.
- **Gemma 2 tech report** (DeepMind, 2024, arXiv:2408.00118): data filtering via
  quality classifiers, knowledge/contrastive data.
- **Common finding**: small high-quality models need **higher code & STEM proportions**
  than naive web distributions, plus dedup and quality filtering. For our 1–2B general
  model we target a *knowledge-dense* mix, not web proportions (see dataset_statistics).

## 6. Deduplication

- **Lee et al. 2022 "Deduplicating Training Data Makes LLMs Better"** (arXiv:2107.06499):
  dedup materially improves performance and reduces memorization. Use exact (hash),
  normalized, and near-duplicate (MinHash/LSH, or embedding similarity) dedup.
- Multi-key dedup (as in Phi-4): dedup on n-grams, on semantic embeddings, and on
  structure independently.

## 7. Context-length distribution

- No single paper prescribes an exact 256..32K bucket split. Evidence from long-context
  pretraining works (e.g., progressive extension in Qwen2/Yi; "YaRN", Peng et al. 2023
  arXiv:2309.00071): **train predominantly on short-to-medium contexts, with a small but
  meaningful fraction of long documents** to induce long-context capability without
  wasting compute. We adopt a log-decaying distribution (see context_length section in
  README/generation_config).

## 8. Model family notes (for teacher routing — hypotheses to benchmark)

- **DeepSeek-V4-Flash** (Hetzner id `DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731`): non-reasoning flash model
  (returns `content` directly, fast). DeepSeek lineage is strong at math/reasoning
  (DeepSeek-Math, R1). Flash variant trades depth for speed/throughput — good for
  high-volume knowledge text and quick fact generation.
- **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-FP8** (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-FP8`): MoE 35B total / 3B active,
  reasoning model (emits `reasoning` then `content`). Qwen family strong at code, math,
  multilingual, structured output. Low active-param cost.
- **GLM-5.2-NVFP4** (`GLM-5.2-NVFP4`): reasoning model, 512K context. GLM family
  (Zhipu) strong general knowledge, Chinese+English, long context.
- **Kimi-K2.7-Code** (`Kimi-K2.7-Code`): Moonshot Kimi lineage, code-focused reasoning
  model. Strong for programming/coding artifacts.

These are **hypotheses**; routing is decided by the benchmark in `reports/teacher_benchmark.json`.
